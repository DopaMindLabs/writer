# Install & offline

LIpsum Writer is a **progressive web app**: you can install it from your
browser so it opens in its own window like any other app, and everything —
the app itself and all your writing — keeps working without a connection.

## At a glance

- **Install** the app from the browser for a dedicated window and icon.
- The app shell is cached on your device, so it **opens and works offline**.
- Updates download in the background and apply **only when you choose**.
- Installing also makes the browser far more likely to grant **storage
  protection** for your work.

## Installing the app

- **Desktop (Chrome, Edge and other Chromium browsers):** look for the install
  icon at the right-hand end of the address bar, or open the browser menu and
  choose *Install*. The app opens in its own window and appears with your other
  applications.
- **Android:** open the browser menu and choose *Add to home screen* (or
  *Install app*).
- **iPhone / iPad (Safari):** tap the share button, then *Add to Home Screen*.

Installing changes nothing about where your work lives — it stays in this
browser profile on this device, exactly as before.

## Working offline

Your writing has always been stored locally, so the missing piece was the app
itself. Once you have visited LIpsum Writer, the app files are cached on your
device: you can open it, browse your spaces, write, and manage notes and
citations entirely without a connection.

Things that talk to the network — such as the optional cloud sync beta —
naturally wait until you are back online.

## How updates arrive

When a new version has been published, the app downloads it in the background
and shows a notice: **"A new version is available"**. Nothing changes until you
choose **Reload** — your writing is saved continuously on this device, so an
update never touches your work. If you ignore the notice, the current version
simply keeps running; the update applies the next time you reload.

## Storage protection

Browsers may clear site data automatically when a device runs low on space.
Because your writing lives in the browser's storage, the app asks the browser
to **protect** its data from that automatic clean-up. You can see the outcome
under **Settings → Export / import → Storage protection**:

- **Protected** — the browser has agreed not to evict this app's data.
- **Best effort** — no promise yet; installing the app, or simply using it
  regularly, makes browsers much more likely to grant protection.
- **Not supported in this browser** — the browser cannot make the promise at
  all; rely on exports and backups.

Protection is not a backup: clearing site data by hand still erases your work,
so keep [exports and backups](your-data) regardless. If you mirror a space to a
sync folder, an installed app also holds on to the folder permission more
reliably between sessions.
